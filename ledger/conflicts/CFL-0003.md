## Classification: Type C1 — scopable (apparent, and caused by our extraction)

Both claims come from **one paper** (`independent_sources: false` holds trivially). `CLM-ARCH-0007` is the 1024-MAC array of the MiX-INT4g16 accelerator (Table V); `CLM-ARCH-0009` is a 512-MAC array of the MiX-INT4 variant (Table VI). Different variant and different size.

They were never one claim. The detector fired because our automated extraction recorded the numbers
under a generic quantity name (`energy`, `power`, `temperature`) with conditions that did not include
the qualifier separating them, so they looked like like-for-like measurements. This is an inconsistency
in **our method**, not in the source, and the user decided how to handle it on 2026-09-21.

## Resolution: `scoped`

- The distinguishing qualifiers, all taken from each claim's own statement (which rests on quotes the
  verifier confirmed are in the paper), are now structured `conditions`. Each claim carries a
  non-public `corrections` entry. Neither claim was published, so no correction notice is required.
- With those conditions the two claims name **different values of a shared condition**, so
  `reconcile.py` treats them as not comparable and detection no longer fires on them.
- Both claims stay `active`.
- The cause is being fixed in the reader (prompt `reader-v3`): a claim with a number must now carry
  the conditions that tell it apart from the paper's other results.
